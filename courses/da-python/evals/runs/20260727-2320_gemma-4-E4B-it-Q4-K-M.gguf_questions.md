## Q15

### Question

For the seaborn `diamonds` dataset: (a) draw a histogram of `price` with 30 bins
and describe its skewness; (b) create `log_price` as the natural log of one plus
price, plot it beside the original, and comment on how the shape changes; (c)
print the average price for each `cut` and draw a box plot of price across cut
levels; (d) draw a scatter plot of `carat` against `price` and say whether the
relationship looks linear; (e) compute the correlation matrix of the numeric
columns, draw an annotated `coolwarm` heatmap, and name the feature most
strongly correlated with price.

### Answer  (231s, 1566 completion tokens, 6.8 tok/s)

Assumptions:
- Dataset: `diamonds` (53940 rows x 10 columns).
- Target/Focus: `price` and `carat`.
- Split ratio: Not applicable, as this is an EDA task.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import seaborn as sns
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Load the dataset using the offline fallback pattern
try:
    df = sns.load_dataset('diamonds')
except Exception:
    df = pd.read_csv('diamonds.csv')

print("--- Dataset Profile ---")
print("Shape:", df.shape)
print(df.head())

# --- Part (a): Histogram of price and skewness ---
print("\n--- Part (a): Price Distribution ---")
plt.figure(figsize=(10, 5))
sns.histplot(data=df, x='price', bins=30, kde=True)
plt.title('Distribution of Diamond Prices')
plt.xlabel('Price')
plt.ylabel('Count')
plt.show()
print("Price distribution is right-skewed, indicated by the long tail towards higher values.")

# --- Part (b): Log transformation and shape change ---
print("\n--- Part (b): Log Transformation ---")
df['log_price'] = np.log1p(df['price'])

plt.figure(figsize=(12, 5))
plt.subplot(1, 2, 1)
sns.histplot(data=df, x='price', bins=30, color='#27ae60', edgecolor='white')
plt.title('Original Price Distribution (Right-Skewed)')
plt.xlabel('Price')
plt.ylabel('Count')

plt.subplot(1, 2, 2)
sns.histplot(data=df, x='log_price', bins=30, color='#2E86C1', edgecolor='white')
plt.title('Log(1+Price) Distribution (More Symmetric)')
plt.xlabel('Log(1+Price)')
plt.ylabel('Count')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
print("The log transformation significantly reduces the skewness, making the distribution appear much more symmetric.")

# --- Part (c): Average price by cut and box plot ---
print("\n--- Part (c): Price by Cut ---")
# Print average price for each cut
avg_price_by_cut = df.groupby('cut', observed=True)['price'].mean().sort_values(ascending=False)
print("Average Price per Cut (Descending):\n", avg_price_by_cut.round(1))

# Box plot of price across cut levels
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 6))
sns.boxplot(data=df, x='cut', y='price', order=df['cut'].unique())
plt.title('Price Distribution Across Cut Levels')
plt.xlabel('Cut Quality')
plt.ylabel('Price')
plt.xticks(rotation=45, ha='right')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# --- Part (d): Scatter plot of carat vs price and linearity ---
print("\n--- Part (d): Carat vs Price Relationship ---")
plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))
sns.scatterplot(data=df, x='carat', y='price')
plt.title('Price vs. Carat')
plt.xlabel('Carat')
plt.ylabel('Price')
plt.show()
print("The relationship between carat and price appears strongly linear; as carat increases, price consistently increases.")

# --- Part (e): Correlation matrix and heatmap ---
print("\n--- Part (e): Correlation Analysis ---")
# Compute correlation matrix for numeric columns
corr_matrix = df.corr(numeric_only=True)
print("Correlation Matrix (Head):\n", corr_matrix.head())

# Draw annotated coolwarm heatmap
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 10))
sns.heatmap(corr_matrix, annot=True, fmt='.2f', cmap='coolwarm', center=0, square=True)
plt.title('Correlation Matrix of Diamond Features')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Name the feature most strongly correlated with price
price_correlations = corr_matrix['price'].drop('price').abs().sort_values(ascending=False)
most_correlated_feature = price_correlations.index[0]
max_correlation_value = price_correlations.iloc[0]

print("\nFeature most strongly correlated with price:", most_correlated_feature)
print("Absolute correlation value:", round(max_correlation_value, 3))
```

Notes:
The price distribution is strongly right-skewed, which is why the log transformation makes the data more symmetric. The box plot shows that while 'Ideal' is the highest quality, it is associated with the lowest average price, which is counter-intuitive due to the effect of carat size. The scatter plot confirms a strong positive linear relationship between carat and price. The heatmap shows that `carat` is the feature most strongly correlated with `price`, confirming the visual assessment.
